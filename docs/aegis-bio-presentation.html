01 · Problem statement

Deep-space emergencies cannot wait 22 minutes for Earth

Beyond Low Earth Orbit, light delay, habitat blackouts, and EVA glove constraints turn cloud-dependent health monitoring into a single point of failure

Light-speed latency and blackouts
JPL Horizons

Mars orbital distance produces 10.52–11.01 min one-way delay (Nov 14–21, 2026) — up to 22 min round-trip before flight surgeons can respond.

Compound habitat and EVA hazards
RadLab + EDA

Elevated cabin CO₂ (2,646 ppm ISS RR-1 vs 908 ppm ground) drives SANS risk, alongside cosmic spikes (369.1 µGy/hr peak) and EVA decompression.

Pressurized EVA glove friction
Human factors

Multi-level desktop menus fail under stiff pressurized gloves or acute hypoxia; crews need immediate local triage directives and one-tap recovery on the wrist.

05 · Scientific and technical validity

Peer-reviewed physiology models verified by automated tests

Deterministic equations in calc.rs adapt dynamically by mission phase and pass all 16 workspace and NASA-dataset tests

NASA-STD-3001 dynamic heart-rate corridors Safe bpm window by phase
Override: out-of-corridor or resting HR + 50 bpm Test suite: 16 / 16 passing
Keytel (2005) energy burn and sweat loss
Metabolic

Computes sex-, weight-, and age-adjusted caloric expenditure (kcal/s, ≥ 1.2 kcal/min basal floor) and evaporative sweat loss (mL/h) against fluid intake.

Haldane N₂ decompression and hypoxia
EVA safety

Integrates tissue nitrogen (t½ = 360 min) and triggers deterministic overrides on R > 1.50, EVA suit pressure < 25 kPa, SpO₂ < 92%, or Shock Index > 0.90.

SANS, osteoporosis, and 60s actigraphy
Long-duration

Combines daily sodium load and cabin CO₂ (SANS tier), resistive workload + calcium + vitamin D3 (bone score), and 60-second sleep/wake epochs.
